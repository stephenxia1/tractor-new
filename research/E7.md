# E7: choosing a play card by card

Decided by the user on 2026-11-19, replacing the whole-move candidate list
(`sim/actions.py` enumeration + `nn/model.py::CandidateScorer`):

- **Every play** (leads and follows) is chosen card by card, then SUBMIT.
- **Fixed pick order**: card types in ascending index order (each type at
  most as often as it is held). Every play is then reachable by exactly one
  pick sequence, so its probability is exact.
- **One PPO action is one whole play**: its log-probability is the sum of
  its picks' log-probabilities. Rewards, the critic and GAE are unchanged.
- **Cold start**, with the **Large** network sizes and **SwiGLU**
  (`nn/arch.py` preset `v7`).

Why: the network can only choose among the moves it is shown. The
candidate list is capped at 128; past 1,024 possible fills of a tractor or
throw lead the simulator offers one hard-coded follow; the engine's list
offers no trick-winning follow in 11.8% of multi-card follows where one is
legal. Card by card has no cap and no hard-coded move.

## Action space

A play is an ascending sequence of card-type picks `t_1 <= t_2 <= ...`
(indices 0..53, the 54-slot card index of `rules/cards.py`) ended by
`SUBMIT` (index 54). At most 25 cards plus SUBMIT: `MAX_PICKS = 26`.

`sim/picks.py` decides which next actions are legal, given the cards
picked so far `P` (counts) and the last type picked `l` (-1 before the
first pick):

- **Lead.** With `P` empty, every held type. After that, any held type
  `t >= l` with copies left, in the same effective suit as `P`; SUBMIT is
  legal once `P` is non-empty. Any one-suit multiset is a legal lead: a
  multi-component one is a throw, and a blocked throw is resolved by the
  rules (only the smallest beatable component is led), in the simulator
  (`PlayState._resolve_throw`) and the engine alike.
- **Follow** (lead of `L` cards, led suit `s`, obligations `ob`). A next
  type `t` is legal iff `t >= l`, a copy is left, `|P| < L`, and `P + t`
  can still be completed to a legal follow using only types `>= t`
  (`rules/follow.py::is_legal_follow`). SUBMIT is legal iff `|P| = L` (and
  then it is the only legal action, because every allowed prefix is
  completable).

Guarantees, tested exhaustively on enumerable positions: no dead ends
(every allowed prefix completes to a SUBMIT); soundness (every reachable
play is legal); completeness (every legal play is reachable, by exactly
its ascending sequence).

A mask is 55 bits; `sim/picks.py` can pack one into a `uint64`
(`pack_mask`), though the rollout buffer stores them unpacked.

The pick space for leads is deliberately wider than the simulator's
`legal_leads`: it allows throws of more than four components and throws
containing singles that are certain to be beaten, which `legal_leads`
filters out. Those are legal under the rules; a blocked throw is
resolved by `PlayState._resolve_throw`, and the component it forces
becomes the lead later follows answer.

## Network

`ModelConfig.action_space = "cards"` replaces the candidate scorer with a
`PickHead`. The history encoder and query block are unchanged in kind; the
query block also returns its final card-type token states `C` (54 x d,
masked to held cards). For pick step `k` with prefix counts `p_k`:

    s_k = h + LN(p_k @ W_p C) + E_step[k]
    u_k = s_k + FFN(LN(s_k))
    logit(t) = <W_q u_k, W_k C_t> / sqrt(d) + b(W_k C_t)     t = 0..53
    logit(SUBMIT) = w_s . u_k + b_s

The encoders run once per decision; each pick runs only the head. In the
update all of a play's steps are scored at once from the stored picks
(teacher forcing). The policy's log-probability of a play is the sum over
its steps of the masked log-softmax at the action taken; its entropy is
the sum of the steps' entropies. A step with one legal action contributes
zero to both.

## Rollout

A card-mode row reuses the buffer's fields: `candidates` holds the cards
picked before each step (float16[26, 54]), `candidate_mask` each step's
legal actions (bool[26, 55]), and `action` the number of steps. The
action at each step is recovered as the difference of consecutive
prefixes (`nn/model.py::pick_actions`); a row is 4.2 KB of these
against 13.9 KB for 128 candidates.

Serial (`train/pick_rollout.py::sample_plays`): the encoders run once
per owner group, then the head once per step for the rows still
picking, with masks from `PickRules.mask`.

Worker pool (`train/parallel.py`): the workers hold the rules. Each
writes, for every row, the masks and prefixes up to the first step that
offers a choice (taking forced steps itself) and that step's index in
`action` (-1 once submitted). The parent runs the encoders once, then
sub-rounds: score every live row's current step, send each worker its
picks (`"pick"`), wait for its `"picked"`. Once every play has
submitted it writes the step counts, log-probabilities and values and
releases the half as before (`"go"`). Works with `--rollout-overlap`.

Checked (`tests/test_cards_mode.py`): every row is an ascending
sequence of legal picks ending in SUBMIT, serial and pool alike, and
the learner scoring the stored picks at the rollout's weights recovers
the stored log-probability to 1e-6 (ratio 1).

## Evaluation

The engine judges every move. The evaluator rebuilds the position, picks
greedily under the same masks and sends the cards (`play` message of
`research/src/evalhost.ts`); the engine resolves blocked throws as the
simulator does. `eval/bots.py` selects this (`candidates="cards"`) for
any card-mode network, so `tractor.cluster eval` and `modal_eval.py`
need no new flag; `train/evaluate.py` does the same against the
reference policy.

## Running it

`--action-space cards` on `tractor.cluster train` or `modal_train.py`,
with `--arch v7`. Parameters in card mode: actor 38.1M, critic 14.8M,
declaration 12.7M, burial 11.0M. A CPU smoke (2 steps of 8 hands, full
hand with declaration and burial phases) peaks at 6 GB.

The loop refuses, at launch, what this version does not do in card
mode: `--compile`, `--cuda-graphs`, `--rollout-forwards` other than
eager, and distillation. `--fixed-shapes` works.

## Not in the first version

Search (top-k plays would come from a beam over picks), expert-iteration
labels, CUDA graphs / compiled forwards for the pick loop, and the app's
TypeScript port. The candidate path stays as it is, selected by
`action_space = "candidates"` (the default).
